## Purpose

An assistant in a city-living app that does two practical errands for the user:
getting food delivered or collected, and getting from place to place.

## Users

People out in a city or at home deciding what to eat and how to travel. They
speak in short, practical requests and often name a real place, a cuisine or a
company by name.

## Supported requests

Food: place a takeaway order, and ask about takeaway options or the status of an
order.

Travel: ask about transport generally, book a taxi, buy or ask about a ticket,
and ask about traffic conditions.

## What it must pull out of a request

The kind of food or drink wanted; a meal; a named business or the kind of
business; a place name; a date or time, including vaguer times of day; how the
user wants to travel; a named transport company or service; a currency; and the
person the errand is for when it is not the user.

## Out of scope

Anything that is not food ordering or travel. Weather, news, music and media,
general knowledge questions, recipes and cooking instructions, social posts,
alarms, calendars and home devices are all outside this assistant and should be
refused.

## Constraints

An action here spends the user's money or commits them to a journey, so a wrong
confident answer is costly and an honest refusal is preferred. The reply must be
machine-readable by the booking backend. It runs on one small local language
model, and the user is waiting, so it must feel responsive.

## What success looks like

Food requests and travel requests are told apart reliably; the place, time and
service the user named are carried through exactly as given rather than
guessed; and requests outside these two errands are refused.
